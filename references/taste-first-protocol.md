# A concrete bet before broad production

Art direction should create a visible difference, not a longer preamble. Keep the first bet compact and provisional until the proof supports it.

```yaml
taste_thesis:
  intended_effect: "what this specific audience should notice or understand"
  content_truth: "approved meaning that must not change"
  dominant_relationship: "observable scale / space / sequence / motion relationship"
  hierarchy: "first, supporting, quiet"
  reference_mechanism: "inspected relationship to borrow, if available"
  restraint: "what not to add; none is allowed"
  trade_off: "what this choice costs; none is allowed"
  proof_question: "what the next rendered comparison should decide"
```

A tension, signature, metaphor, or dominant gesture is useful when it clarifies the work. It is not compulsory for every interface or local edit. Do not invent a business tension, hide needed information, or force a polarizing move to fill the template. Risk is allowed where useful; quality does not require risk.

Translate “premium”, “warm”, “cinematic”, or “technical” into decisions: title width and line breaks, subject scale, image crop, light direction, material behavior, type contrast, pause length, camera relationship, or sound placement. Name the effect, then make the proof show it.

Before listing generic defaults, inspect the brief and references. Identify only the one or two defaults likely to cause a concrete failure here. Do not prime every task with the same list of purple gradients, glass, and bento cards. If the visual vocabulary remains vague, consult the explicitly hypothetical [examples](examples.md), not as style templates.

Use [visual-director-board.md](visual-director-board.md) when several choices must stay coherent. Then render comparable A/B proofs through [visual-feedback-loop.md](visual-feedback-loop.md). Small code sketches and renderer experiments are allowed before a lock; broad implementation waits until the risky visual choice has been inspected. A direction statement alone does not lock a style.
