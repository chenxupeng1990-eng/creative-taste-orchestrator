# Medium-specific proof

The core loop is shared, but evidence must match the medium. Reuse [web art direction](web-art-direction.md), [motion art direction](motion-art-direction.md), and [review application](review-application.md).

Web: inspect first view, substantive content, key interactions, and responsive states. Render matched A/B evidence; test real controls rather than inferring from screenshots.

Motion/video: compare the same beat or action, not necessarily identical timecodes. Watch sound-on and muted, inspect cuts and key frames, then preserve the selected source/timeline version. A contact sheet cannot establish rhythm, sound synchronization, or frame continuity.

PPT: separate visual KV pages from information pages; compare the same argument, evidence and viewing size. Protect approved claims, title conclusions and page-to-page narrative. A visually quieter information page may be the right answer.

Commerce: freeze purchase reasons, evidence order and product identity. Use real product references without inventing packaging, claims or price. Compare first-screen comprehension and a proof section. Do not make consultation or checkout controls pretend to submit when no backend exists.

Brand systems: inspect at least the applications that carry the intended identity; adapt, rather than force identical layouts across channels. Logos and familiar conventions remain useful when correctly applied. Novelty is not an originality/IP clearance test; uncertain asset rights require human review before publication.

For every medium, save the actual source/asset version, capture dimensions/states or beat locators, output bytes and the review decision. A proposed path is not evidence. Working selection remains distinct from final delivery, human preference confirmation and publication.
